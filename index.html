Frontline managers and AI

A technology company wanted to know how frontline managers have to change as AI reshapes their teams. As a subcontractor on the research team, I coded interview transcripts against strict outcome criteria.

I built the analysis top-down, so the client team saw the answer first and the evidence underneath it.

Result: analysis decks the client team reviewed and built on.

Sources

  1. Interview transcripts
  2. Client outcome criteria
  3. Coding framework built for the study

Congressional district intelligence reports

Districts produce a huge amount of public data, and almost nobody puts it together. Candidates end up running on instinct about their own district.

I built a repeatable method that pulls district conditions from federal data and pairs them with voting records, with a citation behind every claim. I ran it on Washington's 4th District first, then on Michigan's 11th as a paid client report.

Result: two full district reports, plus short profiles and strategy briefs.

Sources

  1. U.S. Census, American Community Survey
  2. Bureau of Labor Statistics
  3. FRED
  4. Congress.gov voting records

Precinct-level targeting system

A congressional campaign needed to know which precincts were worth its limited field hours. The certified results that could answer that were split between a state API and a stack of GIS files.

I joined them to district geography and built the general election case for Yakima and Benton counties. The same setup fed volunteer task assignments and research automations.

Result: precinct-level targeting that drove field and fundraising decisions.

Sources

  1. VoteWA results API
  2. WA Secretary of State GIS files
  3. Certified precinct results

Turnover forecasting model

A global manufacturer only knew its attrition number after people had already left, so retention money went wherever the loudest complaint was.

I built a forecasting model in Python on consolidated Workday and survey data, and tested which signals actually predicted turnover. Along the way it showed which data the company had been collecting and never using.

Result: about 85% accuracy, in use for workforce planning.

Sources

  1. Workday HCM
  2. Employee survey data
  3. Python models

Compliance reporting rebuild

EEO and compliance reporting took two months of manual work, so the numbers were stale by the time anyone read them.

I rebuilt it as an automated pipeline with the validation built in, so the whole thing could be rerun whenever someone had a new question.

Result: two months down to twenty hours, ready for an auditor on demand.

Sources

  1. Workday Prism Analytics
  2. EIBs and HR compliance data

Executive dashboard rebuild

Leadership reporting took about thirty hours to prepare, so it rarely happened and follow-up questions went unasked.

I rebuilt it in Power BI on one consolidated data model. Once a report took minutes, leaders started asking second and third questions.

Result: thirty hours of prep down to under ten minutes.

Sources

  1. Power BI, with M and DAX
  2. Consolidated Workday data model

Eleven-country HR and finance integration

An international nonprofit ran HR, payroll, and finance in eleven countries on systems that disagreed about almost everything, including what counted as a headcount.

I led the move to one reporting picture. That meant settling the definitions first, then the technology, and working with auditors on compliance reporting across countries.

Result: one clean reporting picture across eleven countries.

Sources

  1. Intacct
  2. FinancialForce
  3. Country payroll systems
