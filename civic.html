Civic Accountability Lab

The District Nobody Bothered to Study

Congressional districts generate an enormous amount of public data, and almost nobody assembles it into something a candidate, a reporter, or a resident can use. That gap is the work.

District intelligence, legislative accountability analysis, and the operational research infrastructure campaigns actually run on. Every figure below traces back to a public source.

Projects

Research that holds up under scrutiny

District Intelligence  •  WA-04

District Needs & Legislative Voting Analysis

A full-length analysis pairing what Washington's 4th District actually needs against how its representation has voted. Sections cover healthcare, infrastructure and water, veterans and military, federal program funding, housing and food security, and political analysis, each grounded in federal data rather than talking points.

Built from Census, BLS, FRED, and Congress.gov sources with every claim traced to a citation, then packaged into an indicator-based accountability report that scores representation against measured district conditions.

District Intelligence  •  MI-11

Michigan 11th District Intelligence Report

The same methodology applied to a second district as a client deliverable, producing a full intelligence report, a condensed district profile, and a strategic brief sized for a candidate's decision-making rather than a research audience.

It demonstrates the model is portable. The pipeline that assembles a district picture is reusable across all 435, which is the point.

Campaign Operations  •  Congressional Campaign

Precinct Targeting & Research Infrastructure

Retained by a congressional campaign for ongoing research, after leading its strategy and operations through the primary. Built a precinct-level targeting and analysis system for the district using the VoteWA results API and Washington Secretary of State GIS-ready files, then used certified precinct results to build the general election viability case for its two largest counties.

On the infrastructure side: VAN setup, a Zoho CRM donor system with ActBlue integration, Make.com automations for opposition research, and a volunteer research department with tiered task structures, so that volunteers with one hour and volunteers with ten both have something useful to do.

Fundraising Research

Donor Pipeline & Profiling Workflow

A 585-donor pipeline assembled from FEC and Washington PDC filings, with a repeatable profiling workflow that turns a name and an employer into a capacity read and a finisher-ready call sheet. Includes suggested ask sizing against federal contribution limits, in-district tie identification, and the talking points a caller needs before they dial.

Public Tools

WA-04 Citizen Toolkit

The research turned outward. A packet built so a resident with no political background can use the district analysis: a one-page summary, a tool to compare their own situation against district data, town hall questions worth asking, contact scripts, and a social media guide.

Accountability research is only useful if the people it describes can pick it up, so this was designed for a reader who is not a professional.

Tooling

Congressional Voting Research Toolkit

The automation underneath the reports. Pulls from Census, BLS, Congress.gov, and FRED APIs, tracks indicators and voting records, and manages the citation trail so district analysis is a repeatable pipeline instead of a one-off research sprint.
