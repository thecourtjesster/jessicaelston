The district nobody bothered to study

Congressional districts produce a huge amount of public data, and almost nobody turns it into something a candidate, a reporter, or a resident can use. That's the work I do as Civic Accountability Lab.

District intelligence, legislative accountability analysis, and the research setup campaigns run on. Every figure traces back to a public source.

Projects

District needs and voting analysis, WA-04

A full analysis of what Washington's 4th District needs, set against how its representative has voted. It covers health care, infrastructure and water, veterans, federal program funding, and housing and food security.

Every claim has a citation. The result is an indicator-based report that scores representation against measured district conditions.

Sources

  1. U.S. Census, American Community Survey
  2. Bureau of Labor Statistics
  3. FRED
  4. Congress.gov voting records

District intelligence report, MI-11

The same method, run for a paying client on Michigan's 11th District. It produced a full report, a short district profile, and a strategy brief written for a candidate's decisions instead of a research audience.

The method works on any of the 435 districts.

Sources

  1. U.S. Census, American Community Survey
  2. Bureau of Labor Statistics
  3. FRED
  4. Congress.gov voting records

Precinct targeting and campaign research setup

For a 2026 congressional campaign in Washington, I led strategy and operations through the primary, then stayed on for research through the general. I built precinct-level targeting from certified results and made the general election case for the district's two largest counties.

I also set up VAN, a CRM tied to ActBlue, research automations, and a volunteer research team with tiered tasks, so a volunteer with one hour and a volunteer with ten both had something useful to do.

Sources

  1. VoteWA results API
  2. WA Secretary of State GIS files
  3. NGP VAN, Zoho CRM, Make.com

Donor pipeline and profiling

A 585-donor pipeline built from federal and state filings, with a repeatable profile process that turns a name and an employer into a giving history, an ask range, and a call sheet a caller can use before they dial.

Sources

  1. FEC filings
  2. Washington PDC filings
  3. Public biographical records

WA-04 citizen toolkit

The district research, rewritten for residents. A one-page summary, a way to compare your own situation with district data, town hall questions, contact scripts, and a social media guide.

Accountability research only helps if the people it describes can use it, so I wrote it for someone with no political background.

Sources

  1. The WA-04 district analysis

Congressional voting research toolkit

The automation behind the reports. It pulls from federal data APIs, tracks indicators and voting records, and keeps the citation trail, so a district analysis is a repeatable pipeline instead of a one-off sprint.
